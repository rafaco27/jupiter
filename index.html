01 · Size and composition

The biggest planet

11Earths across

Jupiter is so large that about 1,300 Earths could fit inside it, and it has more than twice the mass of all the other planets combined.

142,984 kmDiameter at equator
318 ×Earth's mass
1,898,130,000,000,000,000,000,000,000 kgJupiter's full mass, about 1.898 × 10²⁷ kg, or roughly 1.9 octillion kilograms (4.2 octillion pounds). That's 2.5 times the mass of all the other planets put together.
1.33 g/cm³Average density (Earth: 5.51)
2.5 ×Earth's gravity at cloud tops

What it's made of: mostly hydrogen and helium, the same ingredients as the Sun. By number of molecules it is about 90% hydrogen and 10% helium. Jupiter has no solid surface.

Juno's gravity data suggests the core isn't a neat solid ball but is partly dissolved into the hydrogen around it.

02 · Distance from the Sun

Five times farther than Earth

778million km (5.2 AU)

Jupiter orbits about 5.2 times farther from the Sun than Earth does. Sunlight takes about 43 minutes to reach it, compared with 8 minutes to reach Earth.

03 · Number of moons

A mini solar system

95official moons

The International Astronomical Union recognizes 95 moons of Jupiter, and astronomers keep finding more small ones. The four big ones in gold below were discovered by Galileo in 1610.

  • Ganymede is the largest moon in the solar system, bigger than the planet Mercury.
  • Io is the most volcanic place in the solar system.
  • Europa likely hides a salty ocean under its ice.
  • Callisto is one of the most heavily cratered objects known.
04 · Temperature and atmosphere

Freezing on top, scorching inside

−145°C at the cloud tops
−145 °C clouds~24,000 °C core

The cloud tops are about −145 °C (−234 °F). Deeper down, pressure squeezes the gas and heats it, and the center may reach about 24,000 °C, hotter than the surface of the Sun.

The atmosphere is mostly hydrogen and helium. Its colorful clouds form three main layers: ammonia ice on top, ammonium hydrosulfide in the middle, and water clouds lowest.

Light stripes are called zones and dark stripes are belts. Winds in the jet streams between them blow at more than 500 km/h.

05 · Unique features

Storms, rings and a giant magnet

  • The Great Red Spot is a storm wider than Earth that has raged for at least 190 years, maybe more than 350. It has been shrinking for over a century.
  • Rings: Jupiter has four faint rings made of dust knocked off its small inner moons. Voyager 1 discovered them in 1979.
  • No surface: there is nowhere to land. Gas just gets thicker until it becomes liquid.
  • Strongest magnetic field of any planet, about 20,000 times Earth's, creating huge auroras at the poles and deadly radiation belts.
  • Fastest spin: a day lasts under 10 hours, which makes Jupiter bulge at the equator.

Visitors so far: Pioneer 10 and 11, Voyager 1 and 2, Galileo, Juno and others. Europa Clipper and ESA's JUICE are on their way now.
